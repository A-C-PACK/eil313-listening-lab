Strategy 13 · Unit 3

Listen for numbers and comparisons

Hear numbers, percentages and comparisons correctly, and understand what they show.

What is it?

Speakers use statistics and comparisons to make a point stronger: "55% own two cars", "twice as long", "fewer deaths than before".

Why does it help?

Numbers are easy to mishear (fifteen/fifty) and easy to forget. Even if you catch the number, you also need to know what it measures and why the speaker uses it.

How to do it

  1. Write numbers as numerals immediately: 91%, $435, 1,000.
  2. Next to each number, write what it measures.
  3. Listen for comparison words: more/less than, twice, increase, drop.
  4. Ask: What point does this number support?

Listen for

Numbers
  • percent (%)
  • half / a third / a quarter
  • a million / a billion
  • fifteen vs. fifty (stress!)
Comparison
  • more / less / fewer than
  • twice as… / three times more
  • the same as
  • compared to
  • increased / dropped by
  • -er than / the most

Tip: Teen or ty? In "fifTEEN" the stress is at the end. In "FIFty" it is at the start.

Also use: S12 Follow problem–solution structure · S11 Separate fact from opinion

Show the words (after you listen)
91% of Australian households own at least one car, and 55% own at least two. Now, there are clear advantages to owning a car. You don't have to spend ages waiting, getting squished onto public transport, spending up to twice as long commuting from A to B.

Notes: 91% households ≥ 1 car · 55% ≥ 2 → point: Australia is car-dependent. PT: up to 2× longer commute → point: an advantage of cars (he is being fair before he argues against them). Remember: NINEty-one, FIFty-five. The stress is on the first part of -ty numbers.

Show a model summary

Sharath Mahendran argues that Australian cities are designed for cars, which keeps people dependent on driving. For example, 91% of Australian households own a car, driving costs a household about $435 a week, and 1,266 people died on the roads in 2023. In contrast, in Singapore 7 in 10 people live within a ten-minute walk of a train station, so services are frequent and reliable. He believes Australia should build its cities around public transport, and notes that young people already make up 62% of public transport users.
