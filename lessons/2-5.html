Strategy 10 · Unit 2

Decode stress and connected speech

Hear the stressed key words, and recognize words that blend together in fast speech.

What is it?

In natural speech, important words are louder and longer (stressed), and small words become short and blend together: "want to" sounds like "wanna", "did you" like "didja".

Why does it help?

Many listening problems are not about vocabulary. You know the words, but you don't recognize them when they are spoken quickly. Training your ear on short clips fixes this.

How to do it

  1. Play a short clip. Write the stressed words first. They carry the meaning.
  2. Play it again and fill in the small words between them.
  3. Compare with the transcript. Notice which words were hard to hear and why.
  4. Slow the video to 0.75× if needed, then try again at normal speed.

Listen for

Common blends
  • going to → gonna
  • want to → wanna
  • kind of → kinda
  • did you → didja
  • a lot of → a lotta
  • them → 'em
Weak forms
  • to → /tə/
  • and → 'n
  • of → /əv/ or /ə/
  • can → /kən/
  • for → /fə/

Tip: Stressed words alone often give you the meaning: "FATHER… very HAPPY".

Also use: S8 Use outline and Cornell notes · S9 Follow cause and effect

See it in this talk

In fast speech, content words (nouns, main verbs, adjectives, numbers) are stressed: louder, longer, clearer. Small words (the, of, can, to, is) become short and blend together. So listen in two passes: catch the stressed words first, then fill in the small words.

  1. Listen to the model clip. What do you notice?
  2. Read the words with the cue words highlighted.
  3. Check the explanation.

Show a model summary

Lara Boyd explains that every time we learn, our brain changes, which is called neuroplasticity. The brain changes chemically, which supports short-term memory, and structurally and functionally, which support long-term learning. From her research on stroke, she has learned that behaviour, especially practice, is the main driver of brain change, and that there is no one-size-fits-all approach to learning. Therefore, she believes we need personalized learning and should study how we learn best.
