Strategy 9 · Unit 2

Follow cause and effect

Hear how one thing leads to another, and show it with arrows in your notes.

What is it?

Science talks explain processes: what causes something and what the result is.

Why does it help?

Understanding cause and effect means you understand why something happens, not just what happens. That is what exams and discussions ask about.

How to do it

  1. Listen for cause-and-effect words: "because", "so", "leads to", "as a result".
  2. In your notes, write the cause → the effect.
  3. Link several steps in a chain: A → B → C.

Listen for

Cause → effect
  • because (of)…
  • so…
  • leads to…
  • causes…
  • results in…
  • as a result,…
  • That's why…
  • if… then…
Effect ← cause
  • is caused by…
  • comes from…
  • is due to…
  • is the result of…

Tip: Not every "so" is a cause. Speakers also use "so" to start a new point (S3). Ask: does one thing make the other happen?

Also use: S7 Recognize research and evidence cues · S6 Listen for definitions and explanations

Show a model summary

Neuroscientist Wendy Suzuki explains that exercise is the most transformative thing you can do for your brain. A single workout immediately increases neurotransmitters, which improves your mood and focus. Regular exercise changes the brain itself: it produces new cells in the hippocampus, which improves long-term memory. As a result, a stronger brain is better protected, so diseases like Alzheimer's take longer to have an effect. She recommends aerobic exercise three to four times a week for at least 30 minutes.

80 words. Your summary can be different. Compare the content, not the exact words.
