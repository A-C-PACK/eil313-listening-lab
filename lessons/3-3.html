Strategy 14 · Unit 3

Take comparison notes (T-chart)

Organize your notes in two columns to compare before/after, problem/solution, or two options.

What is it?

A T-chart is a page with two columns. You put matching information side by side, for example the street before and after a change.

Why does it help?

When a speaker compares, a list of notes hides the comparison. Two columns show it clearly and make it easy to explain later.

How to do it

  1. Before listening, decide what is being compared and write two headings.
  2. While listening, put each detail in the correct column on the same row as the detail it matches.
  3. After listening, add a conclusion under the chart: what does the comparison show?

Listen for

Comparison signals
  • before / after
  • used to… now…
  • in contrast
  • while / whereas
  • similarly
  • instead of

Tip: Leave space. Speakers often add the matching detail for the other column a few seconds later.

Also use: S12 Follow problem–solution structure · S13 Listen for numbers and comparisons

See it in this talk

When a speaker compares two things, a T-chart shows the comparison clearly. Listen for the signal that two things are coming: two trajectories… The first… The second…

Show the words (after you listen)
These communities typically face two trajectories. The first is a downward spiral, where political and financial disinvestment causes hypervacancy and decay that pushes people out of a neighborhood. … The second trajectory is gentrification, where developers are able to capitalize off of this distress by buying undervalued properties,… and then renting or reselling them at much, much higher costs, causing displacement.

T-chart: Downward spiral | Gentrification. Row 1 (what happens): disinvestment → empty, decay | developers buy cheap, sell high. Row 2 (result): people leave; banks see risk | legacy residents displaced. Both columns end badly for residents, and that is her point. She wants a third way.

Show a model summary

Bree Jones explains that neglected Black neighborhoods in Baltimore usually face two bad paths: a downward spiral of empty homes, or gentrification, which pushes legacy residents out. Investors told her there was no demand for homes there. In contrast, her non-profit Parity found future homeowners through social networks, renovated abandoned homes and sold them at affordable prices. Within two years it had more demand than homes, which shows that development without displacement is possible.

74 words. Your summary can be different. Compare the content, not the exact words.
