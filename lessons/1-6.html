Exam practice: beyond the words

On the final exam, many questions ask what a speaker means or why they say something. The answer is not in the words alone: think about what comes before and after.

Watch out for these wrong answers: ideas the speaker did not say; answers that are too strong (“all”, “always”, “only”); the opposite of the speaker's view; and answers with the right words but the wrong meaning.

Show a model summary

Yassmin Abdel-Magied, a Sudanese-Australian engineer, argues that we all have unconscious bias and that it limits people's opportunities. She gives evidence such as blind orchestra auditions, which made women 50 percent more likely to pass the first stage, and an Australian study where people with Chinese or Middle Eastern names had to send many more job applications. She says that bias is not an accusation, but something we must identify and acknowledge. Her solution is that each of us should mentor someone who is different from us, to open doors for them.

92 words. Your summary can be different. Compare the content, not the exact words.
