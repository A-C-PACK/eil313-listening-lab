Strategy 3 · Unit 1

Recognize signpost language

Notice the words that tell you where the talk is going.

What is it?

Signposts are words and phrases that show the organization of a talk: a new point, the next step, a list, or the end.

Why does it help?

Signposts are like road signs. If you hear "there are three ways", you know to listen for three things, and you can organize your notes.

How to do it

  1. Listen for numbers and list words: "three ways", "first", "another".
  2. When you hear a signpost, start a new line in your notes.
  3. Use the signposts to make a simple outline of the talk.

Listen for

Introducing a topic
  • Today I want to talk about…
  • Let me tell you about…
  • Here's the thing:
Listing and sequencing
  • There are three ways…
  • First… / Second… / Finally…
  • Another (thing)…
  • Then…
  • Next…
Moving on or concluding
  • Now,…
  • So,…
  • The key here is…
  • In the end,…
  • To sum up,…

Tip: Many TED speakers use a story signpost: "I was in…" or "When I was 11…". That tells you a story or example is starting.

Also use: S1 Predict before you listen · S2 Listen for the main idea (gist)

Exam practice: beyond the words

On the final exam, many questions ask what a speaker means or why they say something. The answer is not in the words alone: think about what comes before and after.

Watch out for these wrong answers: ideas the speaker did not say; answers that are too strong (“all”, “always”, “only”); the opposite of the speaker's view; and answers with the right words but the wrong meaning.

Show a model summary

Julien Bourrelle argues that culture shapes how we behave and how we understand other people's behaviour. He says we all see the world through "cultural glasses". For example, a Spanish tour guide thought his Norwegian friend was not interested because his face was neutral. He believes that if we learn about cultural differences and adapt, we can benefit from diversity, which research shows makes groups more creative and successful.
