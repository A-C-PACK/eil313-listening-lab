Strategy 14 · Unit 3

Take comparison notes (T-chart)

Organize your notes in two columns to compare before/after, problem/solution, or two options.

What is it?

A T-chart is a page with two columns. You put matching information side by side, for example the street before and after a change.

Why does it help?

When a speaker compares, a list of notes hides the comparison. Two columns show it clearly and make it easy to explain later.

How to do it

  1. Before listening, decide what is being compared and write two headings.
  2. While listening, put each detail in the correct column on the same row as the detail it matches.
  3. After listening, add a conclusion under the chart: what does the comparison show?

Listen for

Comparison signals
  • before / after
  • used to… now…
  • in contrast
  • while / whereas
  • similarly
  • instead of

Tip: Leave space. Speakers often add the matching detail for the other column a few seconds later.

Also use: S13 Listen for numbers and comparisons · S12 Follow problem–solution structure

Show a model summary

Janette Sadik-Khan explains how New York City changed its streets quickly and cheaply using paint and temporary materials. Before the change, Times Square was dangerous and chaotic, but after part of Broadway was closed to cars, traffic moved better, it was safer and new stores opened. In DUMBO, a dingy parking lot became a public space, and retail sales rose by 172 percent. The city also built 350 miles of bike lanes, and protected lanes cut injuries by 50 percent. She concludes that changing streets can be fast, cheap and popular.
