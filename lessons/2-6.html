Strategy 11 · Unit 2

Separate fact from opinion

Decide whether the speaker is reporting evidence or giving their own view, and how certain they are.

What is it?

Speakers mix facts (things that can be checked: research, numbers, events) with opinions (beliefs, judgments, recommendations). Hedging words show how certain they are.

Why does it help?

Critical listeners don't accept everything they hear. You need to know what is proven, what is suggested, and what is just the speaker's belief.

How to do it

  1. Ask: Could this be checked with evidence? If yes, it is probably a fact or a claim about a fact.
  2. Listen for opinion signals: "I think", "I believe", "should".
  3. Listen for hedges ("may", "might", "suggests") and boosters ("clearly", "proves").
  4. Mark your notes: F = fact, O = opinion.

Listen for

Opinion signals
  • I think / I believe…
  • In my view,…
  • We should / must…
  • It's wrong to…
  • I worry that…
Hedges (less sure)
  • may / might / could
  • suggests
  • it seems
  • probably
  • some people
Boosters (very sure)
  • clearly
  • proves
  • definitely
  • always / never
  • the fact is

Tip: A strong opinion can be based on facts. The question is whether the speaker gives the evidence.

This is also the Unit 2 capstone. In Listen 2 you will use all the Unit 2 strategies, with less help.

Show the words (after you listen)
One reason that in our lives we spend so much time in the performance zone is that our environments often are, unnecessarily, high stakes. We create social risks for one another, even in schools which are supposed to be all about learning … many students in elementary schools through colleges feel that if they make a mistake, others will think less of them. No wonder they're always stressed out and not taking the risks necessary for learning.

O: "unnecessarily" is his judgment, and "supposed to be" shows what he thinks schools should be. "No wonder…" is his conclusion. Claim: "many students… feel that…" could be checked with a survey, but he gives no evidence here. Compare 4:03: "research shows that… performance usually plateaus". That is an evidence cue (S7), so it is a fact (a research finding).

Exam practice: beyond the words

On the final exam, many questions ask what a speaker means or why they say something. The answer is not in the words alone: think about what comes before and after.

Watch out for these wrong answers: ideas the speaker did not say; answers that are too strong (“all”, “always”, “only”); the opposite of the speaker's view; and answers with the right words but the wrong meaning.

Show a model summary

Eduardo Briceño explains that many people work hard without improving because they spend almost all their time in the performance zone, where they try to do their best and avoid mistakes. In the learning zone, by contrast, we practise what we haven't mastered and expect to make mistakes. For example, Demosthenes practised his speeches with stones in his mouth, and research shows that performance usually plateaus after a couple of years at work. In his opinion, schools and workplaces are often unnecessarily high-stakes, so he recommends creating "low-stakes islands" where mistakes are safe.
