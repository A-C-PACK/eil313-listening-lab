Strategy 1 · Unit 1

Predict before you listen

Use the title, pictures and what you already know to guess what you will hear.

What is it?

Before you press play, you make guesses about the topic, the speaker's message and the words you might hear.

Why does it help?

Your brain understands faster when it already has an idea of what is coming. When the talk starts, you check your guesses instead of starting from zero.

How to do it

  1. Read the title and the information about the speaker.
  2. Ask yourself: What do I already know about this topic?
  3. Guess 2–3 things the speaker will talk about and some words you might hear.
  4. While you listen, check your guesses: right, wrong, or partly right?

Listen for

Look at
  • the title
  • the speaker's job and country
  • the first 30 seconds
  • the pictures and slides
Speakers often say
  • Today I want to talk about…
  • I'm going to tell you a story about…
  • My question is…

Tip: A wrong guess is still useful. You notice the difference, and that helps you remember.

See it in this talk

Good listeners don't start from zero. They guess what is coming, and then listen to check. You can predict before a talk (from the title) and also during a talk: pause, guess what comes next, then check.

When I was 11, I remember waking up one morning to the sound of joy in my house. My father was listening to BBC News on his small, gray radio. … "The Taliban are gone!" my father shouted. … "You can go to a real school now," he said.

The title told us: Afghanistan, girls, education. In the first 40 seconds we hear When I was 11 (a personal story is starting), the Taliban and school. So our prediction is confirmed: this will be her own story about school. Now we can predict the next part: what was school like for her under the Taliban?

Show a model summary

Shabana Basij-Rasikh grew up in Afghanistan when the Taliban made school illegal for girls. For five years she dressed as a boy and went to a secret school, and her father and grandfather supported her education despite the risks. She later graduated from college in the USA and started SOLA, a boarding school for girls. Her main message is that Afghan girls can succeed when their families, especially fathers, support them, and that this gives hope for the future.

79 words. Your summary can be different. Compare the content, not the exact words.
