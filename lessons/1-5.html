Strategy 5 · Unit 1

Take notes with key words and symbols

Write only the important words. Use abbreviations and symbols to save time.

What is it?

Good lecture notes are not full sentences. They are key content words (nouns, verbs, numbers, names) plus symbols and abbreviations.

Why does it help?

People speak about 150 words per minute, and nobody can write that fast. Key words and symbols let you keep up and still understand your notes later.

How to do it

  1. Write nouns, main verbs, numbers and names. Skip small words (the, is, of).
  2. Use symbols: → (leads to), = (is/means), ↑ (increase), ↓ (decrease), + (and), ≠ (is not), ? (question).
  3. Use short forms: b/c (because), w/ (with), w/o (without), e.g. (example), ppl (people), gov (government).
  4. Right after listening, add anything you remember and fix words you can't read.

Listen for

Symbols
  • → leads to / causes
  • = is / means
  • ≠ is different from
  • ↑ more, increase
  • ↓ less, decrease
  • + and, also
  • vs. compared to
  • ? unclear / question
Short forms
  • b/c because
  • w/ with
  • w/o without
  • e.g. for example
  • ppl people
  • info information
  • edu education
  • gov government
  • % percent

Tip: Make your own symbols for words that come up again and again in a talk, for example "C" for culture.

Show a model summary

Hans Rosling explains that the washing machine was a "miracle" for his family because it gave his mother time to read and study. Today only about two billion people have washing machines, and most women in the world still wash by hand. The richest billion people use half of the world's energy, and energy use will grow as poorer countries develop. He argues that rich countries should use energy more efficiently and produce green energy, instead of telling poor people not to want washing machines.

85 words. Your summary can be different. Compare the content, not the exact words.
