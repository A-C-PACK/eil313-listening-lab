Strategy 6 · Unit 2

Listen for definitions and explanations

Catch how a speaker explains a new term, and write the term and its meaning.

What is it?

Academic speakers often introduce a key term and then explain what it means, sometimes with an example.

Why does it help?

Key terms carry the main ideas of a talk. If you miss the definition, you can lose the whole argument.

How to do it

  1. Listen for definition signals: "is called", "means", "in other words".
  2. Write the term, then = and a short meaning in your own words.
  3. Add an example if the speaker gives one.

Listen for

Definition signals
  • X is…
  • X means…
  • This is called X.
  • What I call X…
  • By X, I mean…
  • In other words,…
  • that is,…
Explanation signals
  • What this means is…
  • Basically,…
  • Let me explain.
  • Here's how it works:

Tip: Speakers often say a key term slowly and stress it. Listen for a word that is said clearly and repeated.

Also use: S1 Predict before you listen · S2 Listen for the main idea (gist)

Exam practice: beyond the words

On the final exam, many questions ask what a speaker means or why they say something. The answer is not in the words alone: think about what comes before and after.

Watch out for these wrong answers: ideas the speaker did not say; answers that are too strong (“all”, “always”, “only”); the opposite of the speaker's view; and answers with the right words but the wrong meaning.

Show a model summary

Carol Dweck explains the difference between a growth mindset, the idea that abilities can be developed, and a fixed mindset, the belief that intelligence cannot change. Her research shows that students with a fixed mindset run from difficulty, while students with a growth mindset learn from their mistakes. She recommends praising effort and strategies instead of intelligence, and using the word "yet". When schools did this, struggling students improved greatly.
