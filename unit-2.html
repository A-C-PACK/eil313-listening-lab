Unit 26 talks · about 6–7 hours

The Human Mind, Intelligence & Memory

What is intelligence? Can you become smarter? How does memory work, and can you trust it? Six researchers and memory experts explain what science says about the human mind.

By the end of this unit

  • I can understand how a speaker defines and explains key terms.
  • I can identify research findings: who did the study, what they did and what they found.
  • I can organize my notes as an outline or in Cornell format.
  • I can follow cause-and-effect explanations.
  • I can recognize words in fast, connected speech.
  • I can separate facts from the speaker's opinions.

Strategies in this unit

  1. S6Listen for definitions and explanations. Catch how a speaker explains a new term, and write the term and its meaning.
  2. S7Recognize research and evidence cues. Notice when a speaker reports a study: who did it, what they did, what they found.
  3. S8Use outline and Cornell notes. Organize your notes into main points and sub-points, then add questions and a summary.
  4. S9Follow cause and effect. Hear how one thing leads to another, and show it with arrows in your notes.
  5. S10Decode stress and connected speech. Hear the stressed key words, and recognize words that blend together in fast speech.
  6. S11Separate fact from opinion. Decide whether the speaker is reporting evidence or giving their own view, and how certain they are.
