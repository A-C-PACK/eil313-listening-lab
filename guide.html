How to use this site

For students first, then for teachers.

For students

  1. Go in order. Each lesson teaches one new strategy and reuses earlier ones. Start with Lesson 1.1.
  2. Follow the 7 steps. Get ready → Learn the strategy → Listen 1: Main idea → Listen 2: Strategy practice → Listen 3: Check and decode → Academic words → Respond and reflect. Each step has one instruction in bold at the top.
  3. Listen 1 is without help. Turn captions off and don't pause. Understanding 60–70% is fine.
  4. Use the tools in Listen 2 and 3. Replay clips, slow the video to 0.85× or 0.75×, and use the interactive transcript in Listen 3.
  5. Your work is saved automatically in this browser. To hand in your notes and summaries, open My notes and print or download them.
  6. Use headphones. A lesson takes about 50–75 minutes. You can stop after any step and continue later.

Teacher guide

The site follows the plan EIL313 Listening Website: Video & Strategy Plan: three units, six talks each, and 16 strategies taught in sequence. Each lesson follows a metacognitive listening cycle (Vandergrift & Goh, 2012): plan and predict, then listen for gist, then a strategy task on short clips, then checking and decoding with the transcript (bottom-up work, Field 2008), then vocabulary, then output and reflection. Each unit ends with a capstone lesson that gives less support.

Ways to use a lesson in class

  • Flipped: students do steps 1–6 at home, then the discussion questions (step 7) open your speaking class.
  • In class: do step 1 (prediction) and step 2 (strategy) together on the projector. Students do Listen 1 and 2 individually with headphones. Check Listen 2 as a class, then assign steps 5–7 for homework.
  • Capstones (1.6, 2.6, 3.6) work well as low-stakes assessments: students hand in their notes and summary.

What students do in each lesson

The Listen 2 tasks practise the new strategy. Every lesson also has a prediction, a gist check, a 4-clip dictation, 6–8 academic words with three practice tasks, a summary with a model and checklist, discussion questions and a can-do self-check.

Collecting student work

Work is saved in each student's browser (there are no accounts). Students print or download their notes, summaries and discussion preparation from My notes. They can also download a backup from My progress.

Technical notes

Videos are embedded from the official TED and TEDx YouTube channels. The site needs an internet connection and must be opened from a web address (for example GitHub Pages, Google Sites or a school server), not as a file on the computer, because YouTube blocks embedded videos on local files.
